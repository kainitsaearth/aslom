Coffee Species

Arabica vs Robusta

Two species dominate the world's coffee supply. They grow differently, taste differently, and serve very different purposes. Here's how to tell them apart.

The two giants.

Of the roughly 130 known species of coffee, two dominate global production: Coffea arabica (Arabica) accounts for about 60–70% of world supply, and Coffea canephora (Robusta) makes up most of the rest. A third species, Coffea liberica — which includes Excelsa — is far rarer and mostly grown in Southeast Asia, including the Philippines.

Why does Arabica taste better?

Arabica plants grow slowly at high altitudes where temperatures are cooler. This slow maturation allows sugars to develop fully in the coffee cherry, producing a more complex and nuanced flavor. Higher acidity in Arabica is not sourness — it is brightness, the same quality that makes a good wine lively on the palate.

Robusta grows fast and at lower altitudes. Its higher caffeine content acts as a natural pesticide, making it tougher and cheaper to grow. But caffeine is bitter, which is reflected in the cup. Robusta is not bad coffee — it has legitimate uses in espresso blends where it contributes crema and intensity — but it lacks the complexity that defines specialty coffee.

"If Arabica is the elegant conversation, Robusta is the loud knock on the door. Both have their place — but for specialty coffee, Arabica is the standard."

What about Excelsa?

Excelsa (now classified as a variety of Liberica) is rare and largely unique to Southeast Asia. It grows at mid-elevations and produces a cup that is distinctly different from both Arabica and Robusta — often described as tart, fruity, and complex with a dark, roasty background.

In the Philippines, Excelsa is grown in select regions of Mindanao and is beginning to attract attention in specialty circles. It is a genuinely unique experience — worth trying if you want to move beyond the Arabica vs Robusta conversation entirely.

Which one is in aslom's coffees?

Our launch bean — Alta Vista Excelsa — is a single-origin Excelsa sourced direct from farmer Boy in Brgy. Alta Vista, Bansalan, Davao del Sur. It's one of the rare exceptions that proves there is life beyond the Arabica vs Robusta conversation — fruity, syrupy, with a sweet finish that surprises most first-time drinkers.
